Trust is the only asset a newsroom truly owns. These are the principles we hold ourselves to.

## Accuracy

We verify facts before publication and attribute claims to their sources. When information is uncertain or disputed, we say so. We distinguish clearly between reporting, analysis and opinion.

## Independence

Our journalists do not accept gifts, payments or favours from the people and organisations they cover. Advertisers have no knowledge of, or influence over, stories before they are published. Editorial and commercial teams are separate.

## Sourcing

We prefer on-the-record sources and name them wherever possible. Anonymity is granted only when a source faces genuine risk and the information is of clear public interest. In those cases we explain why anonymity was granted.

## Corrections

When we get something wrong, we fix it quickly and transparently. Substantive corrections are noted at the bottom of the article with the date of the change. Minor typographical fixes are made silently. To report an error, see our [Contact page](/contact/).

## Artificial intelligence

We may use AI tools to assist with research, transcription and routine tasks. Every published article is written, reviewed and approved by a human journalist who is accountable for its content. We do not publish AI-generated articles.

## Advertising

Advertisements are clearly labelled and visually distinct from editorial content. We do not publish sponsored content that could be mistaken for news. See our [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for how advertising partners use data.

## Images

Photographs are not digitally altered beyond standard cropping and colour correction. Illustrations and composite images are labelled as such.

## Complaints

If you believe we have breached these standards, contact **corrections@example.com**. We aim to respond within five working days.
